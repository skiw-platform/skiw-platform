## 1. Goal
The owner moves the host vendor from iCore Technology Sdn. Bhd. to Hostinger. Both the production VPS and the staging VPS go to Hostinger, in a Malaysian data centre (the city and site are confirmed in writing, G-VENDOR item 1), on Ubuntu 24.04 LTS. The host design does not change: Compose, Caddy, nftables, SSH rules, secrets, backups and health checks do not depend on the vendor. What changes is the vendor name, the plan names and prices, the data centre, the due-diligence questions (G-VENDOR) and the list of services that hold data (UNI-11). Hostinger is a new service that holds all data, so this needs a reviewed spec change and a DECISIONS entry before any host is bought (UNI-11, D19).

## 2. Non-goals
- No change to D1–D2, D5–D11, D16, D18, D25, D26, to any host script, Compose file, env file or test. **One conditional exception:** if the owner picks KVM 1 for staging, the staging memory caps in `infra/host/env/staging.env` drop by at least 256 MiB (decision 3), in a PR of their own with the Compose config tests. A search of tracked files found iCore only in docs (spec, plan, runbooks, `agents/PROJECT.md`, DECISIONS, the hosting paper).
- No claim that Hostinger is PDPA-compliant. Its processor terms are a G-VENDOR item.
- No decision on the backup cadence, the off-site copy location, or key custody (Q34 to Q37 stay open).
- No decision on the plan size or contract term. They stay owner-open (Q43, Q44 below).
- No purchase and no contact with Hostinger. The owner does both.

## 5. Flows
- **Buying (owner):** the owner opens the Hostinger account, chooses the plan, a **Malaysian location** and **Ubuntu 24.04**, **turns automatic backups and snapshots off** if Hostinger allows it (decision 9), and buys. The builder does not touch the account.
- **Written confirmation (owner and builder):** the owner sends the eleven G-VENDOR questions to Hostinger in writing. The builder records the answers in `docs/DECISIONS.md`, each marked accepted, mitigated (with the mitigation) or blocking (M1k). Any blocking answer returns the hosting choice to the owner (Q38).
- **If Hostinger answers "blocking":** the host stays a test box with synthetic data. The design needs no change to move to another vendor, except this document's text changes (D24).
- **Failure path:** if a Malaysian location is not offered for the chosen plan at purchase time, the owner does not buy; the choice returns to the owner. No location outside Malaysia is accepted. If Hostinger's written answer names a city or site other than the one the owner expected, the answer is recorded and the owner decides.

## 6. Permissions
Unchanged. The operator is Charlie (the owner). Break-glass access to the production host, the database and the Hostinger client portal stays with the operator and one named second person (Q19).

**G-VENDOR, as it will read (eleven items, same count):** (1) the country, **city and site** of our VPS (a Malaysian location) and of any backup or snapshot copy; (2) a static public IPv4 that survives reboot, rebuild and plan change; (3) backup and snapshot options — **are automatic backups on by default, and can they be turned off for each VPS**; location, retention, deletion on termination, price; (4) SLA — credits, support hours, contact path, response times (the uptime figure on the vendor page is a claim, not a term [A]); (5) terms — data ownership, termination notice, data deletion and disk wipe on termination and on re-provisioning, refund and term rules; (6) whether processor terms or a DPA exist (PDPA), the **contracting entity and its governing law**; (7) security practice — hypervisor and tenant isolation, DDoS protection, staff access to guest disks, **and whether staff or systems outside Malaysia can reach guest disks, the console or the control panel**; (8) resource guarantees — dedicated or shared CPU and RAM, no overselling; (9) client portal or console 2FA, API, rescue console; (10) whether **Ubuntu 24.04 LTS is offered as a clean image**, and whether LUKS or a custom image is possible; (11) whether staging and production can sit in separate client accounts.

## 10. Open questions
- **Q43 — plan size:** KVM 1 or KVM 2 for production, and for staging? Facts you need: KVM 2 = 2 vCPU, 8 GB, 100 GB; KVM 1 = 1 vCPU, 4 GB, 50 GB (decision 3). The iCore sizing had 4 and 2 cores, so CPU is halved; G-LOAD on the production plan is the CPU test, and KVM 4 is the fallback if it fails. On RAM, KVM 2 passes for production; KVM 1 for staging passes only if the staging caps drop by at least 256 MiB. My proposal: KVM 2 for production, KVM 1 for staging with lower caps.
